Overview

A small vision-language model can caption images on a board as cheap as the Jetson Nano 2 GB, but it is slow to start. Most of the wait is spent inside the vision encoder, before the language model writes a single word.

SmolEdgeVLM shortens that wait without retraining the pretrained encoder or language model. After the third encoder layer it merges the 1,024 image tokens into 256, so the remaining nine layers do a quarter of the work. Merging this early damages the features, so a 0.3 M-parameter module, R, repairs them. R needs no captions: it learns only to reproduce what the untouched encoder would have produced.

On the board the first word arrives 42% sooner. The retrained version (v2) scores level with LFM2-VL-450M, a model twice its size, while finishing a caption in about a third of the time.

What this work contributes

  1. A stage-by-stage timing of small captioning models on a 2 GB board, showing where the time goes.
  2. An early token merge inside the frozen encoder, with a label-free repair module. The repair, not the merge layout, is what makes early reduction work.
  3. An evaluation on the board itself against eight compact models, and a drop-in test inside four SmolVLM models.

Problem Statement

To develop a computationally efficient SmolVLM-based image captioning system for resource-constrained edge devices for real-time visual understanding and automated image description, by optimizing vision encoder inference through token reduction, feature selection, and redundant computation elimination without retraining pretrained models, while maintaining caption quality and reducing inference latency and memory usage.

Why the vision encoder

The target board is the Jetson Nano 2 GB. Its CPU and GPU share 2 GB, and once the runtime is loaded about 0.5 GiB is left for the model, enough for roughly 250 M parameters at 16-bit. On this board a SigLIP-B/16 + SmolLM2-135M captioner, the same parts SmolVLM is built from, waits 2.5 s for its first word. 71% of that time is spent in the vision encoder, which carries 1,024 tokens through all twelve of its layers.

Most token-reduction methods remove tokens after the encoder or inside the language model, so the encoder itself still runs at full cost. Removing tokens inside the encoder saves that cost, but it hurts caption quality. Recovering that quality without retraining the large pretrained parts is the core problem.

Objectives

  1. To analyze the vision encoder bottlenecks in SmolVLM for edge-based image captioning applications. Addressed in Methodology, step 1 and Evaluation, latency.
  2. To optimize vision encoder inference using visual token reduction and feature selection without retraining pretrained models. Addressed in Methodology, steps 2 to 5 and Evaluation, reduction against selection.
  3. To evaluate the optimized model in terms of inference latency, memory consumption, computational efficiency, and caption quality for real-time image description on edge devices. Addressed in Evaluation, one part per criterion.

Methodology

The full pipeline. Blue blocks are frozen. Orange blocks are trained. The green LoRA is optional and merged into the weights before deployment.

1. Find where the time goes

Before changing anything, every stage of the captioner was timed on the board: image preprocessing, encoder layers, connector, the language model's first pass over the prompt, and each new word. The encoder dominated the time to the first word, and within it the cost scales with the number of tokens each layer carries. That points to one lever: carry fewer tokens through most of the encoder.

Because R learns from the encoder itself, it can be trained for any model that uses this encoder, in about six minutes on one GPU.

4. Generate the caption

A connector of 0.78 M parameters pools the grid to 256 tokens and maps them into the frozen SmolLM2-135M. An optional LoRA adapter tunes the language model for captions and is merged into its weights, so it adds no time. In total 229 M parameters run on the board, of which 1.08 M are trained, plus the merged LoRA. The first version trained the connector on 5,000 COCO images. Version 2 retrains R, the connector and the LoRA on 127,602 images from COCO, TextCaps and VizWiz, with darkened, blurred, noisy and compressed copies mixed in.

5. Reuse the method inside existing models

SmolVLM and SmolVLM2 use the same encoder, so the merge, the bias and the unmerge fit after their layer 3 as well. Only a new R is trained for each model, and every other weight stays as released.

Experimental setup

  • Board: Jetson Nano 2 GB at 10 W, 16-bit, one image at a time, at most 40 new words. Every model runs through the same PyTorch runtime written for the board. Models of 450 to 500 M parameters only fit with 8-bit weights.
  • Training: one Tesla T4. The encoder and the language model stay frozen throughout.
  • Test data: COCO Karpathy test (5,000 images), Flickr30k test (1,000), nocaps and TextCaps validation, and 100 COCO images that every model captions on the board. None of these are used for training.
  • Metrics: CIDEr, METEOR and SPICE for quality, CHAIR for objects named that are not in the image, time to the first word, time per word and peak memory on the board.
  • Fairness: every model gets the same images, prompt and settings, and every caption is scored on its first sentence with stock openings such as "In this image we can see" removed. Differences come with 95% bootstrap intervals.

The merge saves time, not memory: R adds 0.3 M parameters and the peak hardly moves. The memory benefit comes from the model's size. It fits at 16-bit, while larger models need 8-bit weights, and on this GPU 8-bit weights double the time per word because the board has no fast integer arithmetic.

Computational efficiency

Layers 4 to 12 process 256 tokens instead of 1,024, a quarter of the work in nine of the twelve encoder layers. On the board their time falls from 1,325 to 251 ms, and the merge and repair together cost under 10 ms. Added to four SmolVLM models with only R trained, the same merge cuts their vision time by 55 to 60% and their time to the first word by 33 to 52%.

Limitations

  • Our model is trained on COCO-style captions, while the other models are used as released, so part of the quality lead is caption style. nocaps narrows this gap but does not remove it.
  • Part of the speed lead comes from short captions. The "At 15 words" column removes that effect.
  • Merging makes the model name more objects that are not in the image. R reduces this but does not remove it.
  • v2 has one training run, and the original results three. One board and 100 images limit the board comparison. Energy has not been measured yet.
  • The merge was tested on one encoder family, SigLIP-B/16 at 512 pixels.
